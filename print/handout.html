Three defences, one system

01

Rainwater collection & slow release

Roof-fed tanks catch excess rain. Sensors watch the level and a valve lets water out slowly – only when the drains downstream have room – while keeping a reserve for toilet flushing and other non-potable uses.

02

Storm-drain waste collection

Mesh basket inserts under hinged manhole covers trap litter before it blocks the drain. Drain sensors flag rising water and possible blockages so crews can act early.

03

Community empowerment

Residents report flooding and blocked drains, join cleanups, and earn reward points once staff verify their contribution.

Smart tank monitoring

Safe automated release

The valve opens only when the tank is high, a set amount is kept for reuse, and the drain downstream has room. A checklist shows exactly why it is open or closed.

Quick things to try

  • Open a tank and drag the simulation slider to 95% – watch the alert and valve respond.
  • Demo Controls → “Storm”: drains rise and valves close themselves to protect the street.
  • Submit a report as Aaliyah, then switch to Devon and verify it – Aaliyah gets 25 points.

Please note

  • All people, homes and readings in the app are fictional or simulated, except the prototype station once its sensor is connected.
  • Your changes stay on your own phone. Nothing is sent to WASA, the Regional Corporation or anyone else.
  • Software alone can’t stop flooding – IFRS supports physical drains, tanks and maintenance.
  • Real valves would be controlled by a local controller with its own safety limits, validated by qualified engineers.
  • No official water-rebate scheme exists yet; the rebate section shows a proposal.
  • Use Settings → Data & privacy → Reset to start the demo over.
